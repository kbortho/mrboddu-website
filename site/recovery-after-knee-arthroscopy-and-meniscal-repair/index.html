Recovery after knee arthroscopy and meniscal repair

Knee arthroscopy is keyhole surgery on the knee. For a torn meniscus (the cartilage cushion in the knee), I either trim away the torn part or repair the tear. The recovery is very different for each, so it helps to know which you are likely to have.

The short answer

Trimming (meniscal debridement)Meniscal repair
CrutchesA few days, if neededUsually about 4 to 6 weeks
BraceNot neededUsually for about 6 weeks
Desk workAbout 1 to 2 weeksAbout 2 to 4 weeks
DrivingAbout 1 to 2 weeksAbout 6 weeks, or once out of the brace
SportAbout 4 to 6 weeksUsually 4 to 6 months

Before surgery, I will tell you whether I expect to repair or trim the tear. Sometimes the final decision can only be made during the operation, once I can see the tear.

On the day of surgery

  • Most people go home the same day.
  • The keyhole cuts are small, and they are closed with stitches or strips and covered with a dressing.
  • You will be given painkillers to take at home, and a physiotherapist will show you how to walk safely and which exercises to do.

Recovery after trimming (meniscal debridement)

  • You can usually put your full weight on the leg straight away.
  • Swelling and discomfort are common for a few weeks. Rest, ice and raising the leg help.
  • Start the exercises your physiotherapist gives you to regain full movement and strength.
  • Most people are back to normal daily activities within a few weeks, but it can take 2 to 3 months for the knee to settle completely.

Recovery after a meniscal repair

A repaired meniscus needs time to heal, so the first few weeks are about protecting the repair.

  • Weight bearing: you will usually be able to put some or all of your weight on the leg with the brace locked straight, using crutches, for about 4 to 6 weeks. The exact plan depends on the type of tear and the repair.
  • Bending: bending is usually limited to 90 degrees for the first 4 to 6 weeks, to avoid stressing the repair.
  • Avoid: deep squatting, twisting and kneeling until I or your physiotherapist say it is safe, usually about 3 to 4 months.
  • Return to sport: usually 4 to 6 months, once your strength, balance and confidence have returned.

Rehabilitation plans after a meniscal repair vary, and there is no single agreed programme. A review of the research found that earlier movement and weight bearing did not increase the risk of a repair failing. Your physiotherapist will follow a programme tailored to your repair.

Will the repair heal?

Most meniscal repairs heal, but not all. A review of studies with more than 5 years of follow-up found that about 1 in 4 repairs failed and needed further treatment. Repairs heal best in younger patients, in tears near the outer edge of the meniscus, which has a better blood supply, and when done soon after an injury. Even so, a repair is worth trying when the tear is suitable, because keeping the meniscus helps to protect the knee from arthritis in the long term.

Get medical advice straight away if you notice:

  • a wound that becomes red, hot or swollen, or leaks fluid
  • a fever or feeling unwell
  • pain or swelling in your calf
  • chest pain or breathlessness

In an emergency, call 999.
