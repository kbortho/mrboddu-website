Why do hip and knee replacements fail?

Most hip and knee replacements work well for many years. A small number develop problems, sometimes many years later. Knowing the reasons and the warning signs helps problems to be found early.

The common reasons

  • Loosening: over many years, the bond between the implant and the bone can weaken. This is the most common reason for revision in the long term.
  • Wear: the bearing surfaces slowly wear. Modern materials wear much less than older ones.
  • Infection: uncommon, at about 1 in 100, but it can happen soon after surgery or years later if bacteria spread from elsewhere in the body.
  • Instability: a hip that dislocates, or a knee that feels wobbly or gives way.
  • Fracture around the implant: usually after a fall, and more likely when bones are weakened by osteoporosis.
  • Stiffness: a knee that does not bend or straighten enough.
  • Arthritis progressing: after a partial knee replacement, arthritis can develop in the rest of the knee.

Warning signs

  • New pain, or pain that is getting worse
  • Swelling or warmth around the joint
  • The joint feeling unstable or giving way
  • Clicking, grinding or squeaking

Get medical advice the same day if your joint becomes hot, red and swollen and you feel unwell or feverish. This can be a sign of infection. In an emergency, call 999.

How problems are assessed

I will examine the joint and compare new X-rays with earlier ones. Blood tests check for infection, and sometimes fluid is taken from the joint with a needle. A CT scan can show the position of the implant and the bone around it. Not every problem needs further surgery, and some are treated with physiotherapy or other measures. See painful or worn joint replacements.

Revision surgery

When further surgery is needed, a revision operation replaces part or all of the joint. See revision hip replacement and revision knee replacement.

Reducing the risk

  • Keep to a healthy weight and stay active
  • Get infections elsewhere in the body treated promptly
  • Look after your bone health and reduce the risk of falls
