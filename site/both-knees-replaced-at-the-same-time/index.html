Having both knees replaced at the same time

If arthritis is bad in both knees, you may wonder whether both can be replaced in one operation. For some people this works well. For others, two separate operations a few months apart are safer. This page explains the choice.

The two options

  • Same day (simultaneous): both knees are replaced under one anaesthetic, in one hospital stay.
  • Staged: one knee is replaced first, and the second knee at a later operation, usually at least three months later, once you have recovered from the first.

The possible benefits of a same-day operation

  • One anaesthetic and one hospital stay instead of two.
  • One recovery period, so less time in total away from work and normal life.
  • Both knees improve together, so the second knee does not hold back your recovery.

Why recovery is harder

After a single knee replacement, you have a “good leg” to lean on while the new knee settles. When both knees are replaced at once, you do not. Getting out of bed, standing up from a chair and walking with a frame or crutches are harder in the first few weeks, and you are likely to need more help at home.

A large Canadian study of more than 37,000 people who had both knees replaced found that, after a same-day operation, about 45 in 100 went on to an inpatient rehabilitation unit rather than straight home, compared with about 9 in 100 after staged operations. Blood transfusion was also about twice as common (37 in 100 compared with 17 in 100).

This is less of a problem with partial knee replacements. A partial knee is a smaller operation, with less blood loss and a quicker recovery, so most people are able to get moving soon even when both knees are done together. See partial or total knee replacement.

The extra risks

Replacing both knees at once is a longer operation, with a longer anaesthetic and more blood loss, and it puts more strain on the heart and lungs. This is why the anaesthetic risk needs careful thought.

  • Total knee replacements: a review of 18 studies and almost 28,000 patients found that a same-day operation on both knees carried about twice the risk of death, a higher risk of heart complications and a higher risk of a blood clot on the lung (pulmonary embolism) than a single knee replacement. These risks are still small in absolute terms. In a large US study, 3 in 1,000 people died in hospital after having both knees replaced, compared with about 1 in 1,000 after one knee. Being over 75 was one of the strongest risk factors.
  • Partial knee replacements: the evidence is more reassuring. A review of 765 same-day partial knee replacements on both sides found that they were as safe as staged operations, and a National Joint Registry study of almost 2,000 matched patients found no difference in the risk of death compared with a partial knee replacement on one side. One UK study did find more serious complications after same-day operations, so careful selection still matters.

Choosing the right patients is the key

Most of the extra risk falls on people who are older or have other health problems. An international expert consensus advised stricter selection for same-day operations, and that safety should always come before convenience. A same-day operation is more likely to suit you if:

  • both knees are badly affected and are holding you back equally
  • you are fit and well, without significant heart or lung disease
  • you are not very overweight
  • you are able to work hard at your exercises, and have good help at home for the first few weeks

Staged operations are usually safer if you are older, have heart or lung problems, or have other health conditions that raise the risks of a long anaesthetic. You will meet the anaesthetist before any decision is final, and they will assess whether a same-day operation is safe for you.

How I help you decide

I will examine both knees and review your X-rays to see whether one or both are suitable for a partial knee replacement, and we will talk through your general health, your home support and what matters most to you. If a same-day operation is not right for you, we will plan the second knee once you have recovered from the first. If you have insurance, check with your insurer before booking, because they may need to authorise both knees in advance.
