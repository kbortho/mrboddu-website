Exercises before and after a hip replacement

Exercise is one of the most important parts of your recovery. Strong muscles around the hip help you walk without a limp, protect your balance and get you back to normal sooner. The physiotherapists will show you these exercises in hospital, and this page is a reminder for home.

Please note: these exercises are for general guidance only. Our physiotherapists will tailor your exercise programme to you, so follow their advice if it differs from this page.

General advice

  • Do your exercises 3 or 4 times a day. Little and often works better than one long session.
  • Start with 5 to 10 repetitions of each exercise and build up to 10 to 15.
  • Some discomfort is normal, but stop if an exercise causes sharp pain.
  • Take your painkillers about 30 to 60 minutes before exercising in the first few weeks.
  • I do not routinely ask patients to follow “hip precautions”. Move freely within comfort, unless I have told you otherwise.

Before your operation

Getting stronger before surgery helps your recovery afterwards. Walking, an exercise bike, swimming or water exercise, and the exercises below are all helpful if your hip allows. It also helps to practise walking with crutches before your operation.

Stage 1: the first days after surgery

These can be done lying on your back on the bed.

  1. Ankle pumps: point your feet up and down briskly for 30 seconds. This helps circulation and reduces the risk of blood clots. Do this every hour while you are awake.
  2. Buttock squeezes: squeeze your buttocks together, hold for 5 seconds, then relax.
  3. Thigh tightening: push the back of your knee down into the bed to tighten the thigh muscle, hold for 5 seconds, then relax.
  4. Heel slides: slide your heel up towards your bottom, bending the hip and knee as far as is comfortable, then slide it back down.
  5. Leg out to the side: keeping your kneecap pointing to the ceiling, slide the operated leg out to the side and back again.

Stage 2: standing exercises

Stand facing a kitchen worktop or the back of a sturdy chair, and hold on with both hands.

  1. Marching: lift your knee up in front of you, as if marching, then lower it. Do not lift it higher than your hip.
  2. Leg to the side: keeping your body upright and your toes pointing forward, lift the operated leg out to the side, then bring it back.
  3. Leg backwards: keeping your body upright, take the operated leg straight back behind you without leaning forward, then bring it back.
  4. Mini squats: with your feet hip-width apart, bend both knees a little, keeping your heels on the floor, then straighten up.

Stage 3: from about 4 to 6 weeks

As you get stronger, your physiotherapist may add:

  • Bridging: lying on your back with your knees bent, lift your bottom off the bed, hold for 5 seconds, then lower.
  • Step-ups: step up onto the bottom stair with the operated leg, then step down, holding the banister.
  • Side-lying leg lifts: lying on your good side with a pillow between your knees, lift the operated leg up towards the ceiling, then lower it.
  • Exercise bike: start with no resistance, and set the seat high so that the hip does not bend too much.

Walking

Walking is an excellent exercise. Increase the distance a little each day. Most people use crutches or a frame for the first few weeks, then a single stick, held in the hand opposite the operated hip, before walking without aids. Try to walk with an even, smooth step rather than a limp.

When to get help

Let me or your physiotherapist know if your pain is getting worse rather than better, if you are not making progress, or if you still have a noticeable limp after about 3 months.
