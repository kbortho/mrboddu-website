Kneeling and bending after a knee replacement

Two common worries after a knee replacement are whether you will be able to kneel, and how far the knee will bend. The good news is that kneeling will not damage your new knee, and most people can kneel more easily than they think.

Can I kneel after a knee replacement?

Yes. Kneeling will not damage or loosen your new knee. Many people avoid it because it feels strange or uncomfortable, often because the skin around the scar is numb or sensitive.

In one study, only 37 in 100 people thought they could kneel after their knee replacement, but when they were asked to try, 81 in 100 could. Kneeling usually becomes easier as the scar settles over the first year.

How to start kneeling

  • Wait until your wound has fully healed, usually after about 6 weeks.
  • Start by kneeling on a soft surface, such as a cushion on a bed or sofa.
  • Use a kneeling pad or gardening kneeler, and something sturdy to hold on to as you get up and down.
  • Kneel for short spells at first and build up gradually.

How much will my knee bend?

Most knee replacements bend enough to manage stairs, sit in an ordinary chair and get in and out of a car. How far your knee bends depends partly on how much it bent before surgery, and partly on the work you put into your exercises in the first few months.

As a guide, we aim for the knee to bend to at least 90 degrees (a right angle) by about 6 weeks, and it often keeps improving for several months after that. Getting the knee fully straight is just as important as bending it, so that you can walk normally.

If your knee feels stiff

  • Do your exercises little and often, 3 or 4 times a day. See knee replacement exercises.
  • Take your painkillers regularly in the early weeks, so that you can bend the knee properly. See pain relief after a knee replacement.
  • Use an exercise bike once your physiotherapist agrees. It is one of the best ways to improve bending.
  • Let me or your physiotherapist know early if the knee is not bending as expected.

If the knee remains very stiff despite physiotherapy in the first few months, a short procedure called a manipulation under anaesthetic can help. While you are asleep, the knee is gently bent to free up the stiffness. Only a small number of people need this.

Numbness around the scar

A numb patch on the outer side of the scar is very common and can make kneeling feel odd. It is harmless, and usually shrinks over time. See clicking or numbness after a knee replacement.
