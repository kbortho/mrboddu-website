Returning to work and flying after a joint replacement

Most people who were working before their operation go back to work. When depends on the type of job you do and how your recovery goes. This page also explains when it is sensible to fly.

Going back to work

Type of workUsual time off
Office or desk work, or working from homeAbout 4 to 6 weeks
Work on your feet, such as retail or teachingAbout 6 to 8 weeks
Heavy manual work, such as building or farmingAbout 3 months

These are guides only. A review of studies found that people returned to work, on average, between about 1 and 14 weeks after a hip replacement, and between about 8 and 12 weeks after a knee replacement. Most people who wanted to work went back.

Tips for a smooth return

  • Talk to your employer before your operation about a phased return, such as shorter days or lighter duties at first.
  • If you work at a desk, get up and walk around every 30 to 60 minutes. This eases stiffness and helps prevent blood clots.
  • Think about your journey to work. If you drive, see driving after a joint replacement.
  • I can provide a fit note (sick note) for the time you need off.

Flying

The main concern with flying is the risk of a blood clot in the leg (deep vein thrombosis), which is higher for a few weeks after surgery. Long periods of sitting still add to that risk.

  • Short flights (a few hours) are usually fine after about 6 weeks.
  • Long-haul flights: it is usually best to wait about 3 months.

Some studies of people who flew home a few days after surgery have not found a clear increase in blood clots, but they were small. Because a clot is a serious complication, I advise waiting where you can. If you need to fly sooner, talk to me first.

To reduce your risk on a flight

  • Walk around the cabin every hour if you can, and do ankle exercises in your seat.
  • Drink plenty of water and limit alcohol.
  • Ask for an aisle seat, and consider booking extra legroom.
  • Check that your travel insurance covers you after recent surgery.

Airport scanners

Your new joint may set off the metal detector at security. You do not need a card or letter. Simply tell the security staff that you have a joint replacement.
