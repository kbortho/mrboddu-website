Partial or total knee replacement?

If arthritis affects only one part of your knee, you may be able to choose between a partial and a total knee replacement. Both are good operations. This page compares them so that you can think about which suits you before we talk.

The difference

A total knee replacement resurfaces the whole joint. A partial knee replacement replaces only the worn part, most often the inner (medial) side, and keeps the healthy rest of the knee and its ligaments.

What the research shows

  • A large UK trial (TOPKAT) found that patients did as well after a partial as after a total knee replacement at five years.
  • National Joint Registry studies and a review published in the BMJ found lower risks of complications, such as blood clots, heart attack, stroke and deep infection, after a partial knee replacement.
  • Partial knee replacements are more likely to need further surgery later. If that happens, a partial knee can usually be converted to a total knee replacement.

National guidance (NICE) recommends that people with arthritis in the inner part of the knee are offered the choice of either operation.

How I help you decide

I will examine your knee and review standing X-rays, and sometimes further X-rays, to see whether a partial knee replacement is possible for you. Where both are suitable, we will weigh up the quicker recovery of a partial knee against the slightly higher chance of further surgery, in the light of your age, activity and preferences. Either operation can be done with Mako robotic assistance, but I use it only where it is appropriate for you.
