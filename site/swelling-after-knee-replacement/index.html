Swelling after a knee replacement: what is normal?

Swelling, bruising and warmth around the knee are a normal part of healing after a knee replacement. They can last longer than many people expect. Here is what is normal and what helps.

What is normal

  • First 2 weeks: swelling and bruising are usually at their most noticeable. Bruising may spread down the leg to the ankle.
  • Up to 3 to 6 months: some swelling is common, especially at the end of the day or after more activity.
  • Up to a year: the knee may feel warmer than the other one. This is normal and settles gradually.

Why it matters

Swelling can make the knee feel stiff and tight, and is linked to weakness of the thigh muscles in the first weeks. Keeping swelling under control makes your exercises easier.

What helps

  • Rest with your leg raised, with your foot higher than your hip, a few times a day
  • Use a cold pack wrapped in a towel for 15 to 20 minutes at a time
  • Keep doing your exercises, and walk little and often
  • Pace your activity: a lot more swelling the next day usually means you did too much

Get medical advice straight away if you notice:

  • Pain, swelling or tenderness in your calf, which can be a sign of a blood clot
  • A wound that becomes red, hot or swollen, or leaks fluid
  • A fever or feeling unwell
  • Chest pain or breathlessness

In an emergency, call 999.
