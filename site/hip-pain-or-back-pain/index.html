Is it hip pain or back pain?

Pain around the hip can come from the hip joint itself, from the lower back, or from the tendons on the side of the hip. They need different treatments, so finding the cause is the first step.

Where you feel the pain

Likely causeTypical features
The hip joint, for example arthritisPain in the groin or the front of the thigh, sometimes spreading to the knee. Stiffness, difficulty putting on shoes and socks, and a limp.
The lower backPain in the buttock or lower back, sometimes spreading down the back of the leg below the knee. Pins and needles or numbness. Pain made worse by bending or sitting for long.
The tendons on the side of the hipPain on the outside of the hip, often worse lying on that side at night, after climbing stairs, or after crossing the legs.

Hip arthritis can cause pain only in the knee, which sometimes delays the diagnosis. Many people have more than one cause at the same time.

How the cause is found

At your consultation, I will ask where and when you feel the pain, and examine your hip, back and knee. Pain when the hip is gently rotated points to the hip joint. An X-ray of the hip is usually enough to show arthritis. Sometimes a scan of the back helps, and occasionally an injection into the hip is used as a test: if the pain goes away for a while, the hip is the likely cause.

Treatment

  • Hip arthritis: exercise and physiotherapy first, then a hip replacement if pain still has a real effect on your life. See hip arthritis.
  • Back-related pain: usually physiotherapy and staying active. If your pain comes from your back, I will explain this and advise on the right next step, which may include seeing a spinal specialist.
  • Tendon pain on the side of the hip: usually a specific exercise programme with a physiotherapist, and sometimes an injection.

Go to A&E or call 999 straight away if back pain or leg pain comes with any of these:

  • Numbness around your bottom or genitals
  • New problems passing urine or controlling your bladder or bowels
  • Weakness in both legs

These can be signs of cauda equina syndrome, which needs urgent treatment.
