How long do hip and knee replacements last?

Most hip and knee replacements last for many years, and for many people they last a lifetime. Here is what the evidence shows, and what affects how long yours may last.

What large studies show

Two large reviews published in the Lancet in 2019 pooled national joint registry data for hundreds of thousands of patients with more than 15 years of follow-up.

  • Hip replacements: about 58 in 100 were still working 25 years after surgery.
  • Total knee replacements: about 82 in 100 were still working 25 years after surgery.
  • Partial knee replacements: about 70 in 100 were still working 25 years after surgery.

These figures include implants that are no longer used, so modern joint replacements may do better.

What affects how long a joint replacement lasts

  • Age: younger people are more active and live longer with their joint, so they are more likely to need further surgery at some point.
  • Activity: walking, swimming, cycling and golf are encouraged. High-impact sports such as running put more strain on the joint.
  • Weight: being overweight increases the load on the joint replacement, so keeping to a healthy weight helps it last.
  • Bone strength: bones can weaken with age, for example from osteoporosis. A fall can then cause a fracture around the joint replacement, which sometimes needs revision surgery. Treating osteoporosis and reducing the risk of falls both help.
  • Infection: uncommon, but it can affect a joint replacement at any time, so get any infection elsewhere in the body treated promptly.
  • The implant and how it is positioned: this is why I use implants with proven long-term results.

Signs that a joint replacement may need checking

New or worsening pain, swelling, a feeling of instability, or clicking and grinding can be signs of wear or loosening. If you notice these, see painful or worn joint replacements. Not every problem needs further surgery, and when it does, I perform revision hip and revision knee replacement.
