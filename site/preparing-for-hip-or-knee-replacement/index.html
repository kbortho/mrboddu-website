Preparing for your hip or knee replacement

Good preparation helps you recover faster and more safely. Before your operation, you will be seen in the pre-assessment clinic at Nuffield Health Ipswich, where you will get advice tailored to you and clear instructions. This guide explains what to expect and what you can do yourself.

The short answer

  • Attend the pre-assessment clinic at Nuffield Health Ipswich and follow its instructions carefully.
  • Keep active and do the exercises your physiotherapist gives you.
  • If you smoke, stop, ideally at least 4 weeks before surgery.
  • Get your home ready and arrange help for the first week or two.

The pre-assessment clinic

A few weeks before your operation, the pre-assessment team at Nuffield Health Ipswich will check that you are ready for surgery and the anaesthetic. They will:

  • ask about your health and any previous operations or anaesthetics
  • check your blood pressure, and arrange blood tests, swabs for infection and, if needed, a heart tracing (ECG)
  • review all your medicines, and tell you which ones to stop or change before surgery, and when. This is especially important for blood thinners, diabetes medicines and some arthritis medicines
  • give you clear instructions about when to stop eating and drinking before your operation

Please bring a list of your medicines, or the medicines themselves, to the appointment. If anything is found that needs treating first, such as anaemia, high blood pressure or poorly controlled diabetes, the team will tell you and, where needed, contact your GP. Treating these problems before surgery makes it safer.

Getting fit for surgery

  • Exercise: a 2023 review of 48 randomised trials found that exercise programmes before surgery (“prehabilitation”) improved strength and function before a hip or knee replacement, and function in the first 6 weeks after a knee replacement. Keep as active as your joint allows. Swimming, cycling and an exercise bike are often easier than walking. See knee replacement exercises and hip replacement exercises, which you can start before surgery.
  • Stop smoking: smoking increases the risk of wound and chest problems. A review of trials found that stopping smoking before surgery reduces complications, and the benefit is greater the earlier you stop. Your GP or pharmacy can help.
  • Alcohol: keep within the recommended limits, and avoid alcohol in the days before surgery.
  • Weight: if you are overweight, losing even a little weight can make surgery and recovery easier. See joint replacement if you are overweight.

Infection prevention

  • Skin: please tell us if you have any cuts, sores, rashes or infections on the leg being operated on, as surgery may need to wait until they heal. Do not shave the leg yourself.
  • Teeth and other infections: if you have a dental problem, a urine infection or any other infection, please tell the team so it can be treated before surgery.

Getting your home ready

  • Remove trip hazards, such as loose rugs and trailing wires.
  • Put everyday items within easy reach, at waist height.
  • Have a firm chair with arms, at a comfortable height.
  • Stock up on easy meals for the first week or two.
  • Arrange for someone to drive you home, and for help at home for the first week or two.
  • Arrange help with shopping, pets and other tasks for the first few weeks. You will not be able to drive for about 6 weeks.

The physiotherapists and occupational therapists will check that you have any equipment you need, such as crutches, before you go home.

What to bring to hospital

  • Your medicines, in their original boxes
  • Loose, comfortable clothes, and shoes or slippers with a back and a non-slip sole
  • Toiletries, glasses, hearing aids and phone charger

Questions before your operation

If you have any questions, please contact my secretary or the hospital. See also your operation and recovery.
