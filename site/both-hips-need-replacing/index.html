Do both of your hips need replacing?

Arthritis often affects both hips. If both are bad enough to need replacing, they can be replaced in two separate operations (“staged”) or in one operation (“simultaneous”). I replace one hip at a time, because it is the safer option for most people.

The short answer

  • I replace one hip at a time, in two separate operations.
  • I usually replace the more painful hip first.
  • Most people have the second hip replaced at least 3 months after the first, once they have recovered well.

What the research shows

  • A study using national hospital data from England compared people who had both hips replaced in one operation with those who had them replaced in two operations. The one-operation group had a higher risk of blood clots in the lungs, heart attack, kidney problems, chest infection and death in hospital, even though they were younger on average.
  • A 2022 review of more than 100,000 patients found that having both hips replaced together reduced the total hospital stay and cost, but increased the risk of blood clots in the lungs and of fractures around the implant.

Some centres replace both hips together in carefully selected, younger and very fit patients. But for most people, I believe the extra risk is not worth the shorter overall recovery.

Why one hip at a time

  • Safety: one operation at a time puts less strain on your heart, lungs and blood supply.
  • Easier recovery: with one new hip, you have a stronger leg to stand on while you recover, which makes walking and getting about easier and safer.
  • Your first hip helps the second: you will know what to expect, and you will be stronger and fitter for the second operation.
  • A chance to reassess: some people find that the second hip troubles them less once the first has been replaced, and they choose to wait.

Which hip first?

I usually replace the more painful or more damaged hip first. We will decide together, based on your symptoms, your examination and your X-rays.

How long between the two operations?

There is no fixed rule. I usually recommend waiting until you have recovered well from the first operation, which is usually at least 3 months. By then, most people are walking without sticks and have regained much of their strength. Some people prefer to wait longer, which is fine. If you had a complication after the first operation, such as a wound problem or a blood clot, we will discuss whether you need extra precautions for the second.

Planning ahead

  • We can plan both operations at your consultation, so you know what to expect.
  • If you have private medical insurance, please check with your insurer how they authorise a second operation, as each one may need its own pre-authorisation.
  • If you are paying for yourself, Nuffield Health Ipswich will give you a fixed-price package for each operation. See the cost of a private hip or knee replacement.
