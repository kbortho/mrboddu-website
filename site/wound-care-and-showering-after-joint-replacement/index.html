Wound care and showering after a joint replacement

Looking after your wound well helps it heal and lowers the risk of infection. This guide explains when you can shower, bath and swim, how the wound is checked, and what to look out for.

The short answer

  • Showering: usually fine from 48 hours after surgery, keeping the waterproof dressing on.
  • Baths, swimming and hot tubs: wait until the wound has fully healed and any scabs have gone, usually after about 3 to 6 weeks.
  • Wound check and clip removal: at about 2 weeks, usually by a nurse at the hospital.

Your dressing

You will usually go home with a waterproof dressing over the wound. Leave it in place unless it is leaking, peeling off or soaked through. If it needs changing, contact the ward or the hospital nurse rather than changing it yourself, so that the wound stays clean.

Showering

National guidance (NICE) advises that people can shower safely 48 hours after surgery.

  • Use a shower rather than a bath at first, and keep the water away from the dressing as much as you can.
  • Pat the area around the dressing dry with a clean towel. Do not rub it.
  • Do not use creams, powders or perfumed products on or near the wound until it has healed.
  • Use a non-slip mat, and a shower seat if you have one. If you have a shower over the bath, ask your physiotherapist or occupational therapist how to get in and out safely.

Clips or stitches

At about 2 weeks, your wound will be checked and any clips or stitches removed, usually by a nurse at the hospital. Some wounds are closed with dissolving stitches, which do not need removing.

Looking after your scar

  • Once the wound has fully healed, gently massaging the scar with an unperfumed moisturiser can help it soften and flatten.
  • Protect the scar from the sun for the first year, with clothing or a high-factor sun cream, as new scars burn and darken easily.
  • Scars usually fade from red to pale over 6 to 12 months.
  • A numb patch beside the scar is common, especially after a knee replacement. It is harmless and usually shrinks with time.

Signs of infection

Contact the hospital straight away if you notice:

  • A wound that becomes red, hot or swollen, or leaks fluid
  • Increasing pain in the joint rather than gradual improvement
  • A fever, shivering or feeling generally unwell

Do not start antibiotics before you have been assessed, unless a doctor has advised it. In an emergency, call 999.
