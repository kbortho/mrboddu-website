Exercises before and after a knee replacement

Exercise is the most important thing you can do to get a good result from a knee replacement. Starting before your operation makes recovery easier. These are the exercises most people are given. Your physiotherapist will tailor them to you.

The four key exercises

  1. Straight leg raise. Lie on your back with your other knee bent. Tighten the thigh muscle of your straight leg, then lift it about 20 centimetres off the bed. Hold for 5 seconds and lower slowly. Repeat 10 times.
  2. Heel slide. Lie on your back with your legs straight. Slide the heel of your operated leg towards your bottom, bending the knee as far as is comfortable. Hold for 5 seconds, then slide it back. Repeat 10 times.
  3. Seated knee bend. Sit on a firm chair with your foot on the floor. Slide your foot back under the chair as far as you can, hold for 5 seconds, then relax. Repeat 10 times.
  4. Knee straightening. Sit with your leg out straight and your heel resting on a rolled-up towel. Let the knee sag towards the bed, or press it gently down, and hold for up to a minute. Repeat 3 times. Do not put a pillow under the knee itself, because the knee can stiffen in a bent position.

Before your operation

Stronger thigh muscles before surgery help you get moving sooner afterwards. As well as the exercises above, try to:

  • Practise standing up from a chair without using your hands, 10 times, a few times a day
  • Use an exercise bike or swim, if your knee allows
  • Keep walking as much as is comfortable

After your operation

  • Start the exercises the day after surgery, or as soon as the physiotherapists show you.
  • Do them 3 or 4 times a day. Little and often works better than one long session.
  • Take your painkillers about 30 minutes before you exercise.
  • Aim to get the knee fully straight, and bend it a little further each week.
  • An exercise bike is a good way to improve the bend from about 4 to 6 weeks.

Some discomfort during exercise is normal. Stop and seek advice if you have sharp pain, or if pain or swelling is getting steadily worse rather than better.
