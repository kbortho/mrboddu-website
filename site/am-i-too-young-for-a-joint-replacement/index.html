Am I too young for a hip or knee replacement?

There is no lower age limit for a hip or knee replacement. What matters is how much arthritis affects your life, and whether other treatments have helped. Age does affect the decision, and this page explains how.

What national guidance says

National guidance (NICE) says that age on its own should not stop someone being referred for joint replacement surgery. The decision depends on your symptoms, how they affect your daily life and work, and the balance of benefits and risks for you.

How age makes a difference

  • Younger people get many years of benefit. Living with severe arthritis pain in your 40s or 50s can affect work, family life, sleep and fitness for many years.
  • Younger people are more likely to need further surgery in their lifetime. They are more active and will live longer with their joint. A large UK study found that the lifetime chance of needing a revision was about 1 in 20 for people having surgery at 70 or over, but up to about 1 in 3 for men having it in their early 50s.
  • Revision surgery is usually possible. If a joint replacement wears out, it can usually be replaced, although a revision is a bigger operation. See revision hip and revision knee replacement.

Making the most of other treatments first

For younger people, it is especially worth trying exercise and physiotherapy, weight loss if needed, changes to activities and painkillers before surgery. A steroid injection can give short-term relief. See steroid injections.

Options that may suit younger people

  • A partial knee replacement, if arthritis affects only one part of the knee
  • Implants with long, proven track records in the National Joint Registry, which I use for my hip and knee replacements

How we decide together

I will explain the likely benefits and risks for you, including the chance of needing further surgery in the future, so that you can make an informed choice. There is no pressure to decide on the day. See when is it time for a replacement?
