Can I have a joint replacement if I am overweight?

Many people with hip or knee arthritis are overweight, and arthritis pain makes it harder to be active. Being overweight should not on its own stop you being assessed for a joint replacement. This page explains how weight affects the decision.

What national guidance says

National guidance (NICE) says that people should not be excluded from referral for joint replacement because they are overweight. The decision depends on your symptoms, how they affect your life, and the balance of benefits and risks for you.

How weight affects surgery

  • The benefits are still large. People who are overweight usually get a big improvement in pain and function after a joint replacement.
  • Some risks are higher. Studies have found higher rates of wound problems and deep infection after joint replacement in people who are obese, and a higher chance of further surgery.
  • The anaesthetic can be more complex, and the anaesthetist will assess this before your operation.

As a guide, a body mass index (BMI) under 35 is generally considered desirable before a joint replacement, because the risks of surgery rise above this. If your BMI is higher, we will talk about whether losing some weight first would make your operation safer.

How losing weight helps

  • Even modest weight loss can ease arthritis pain. A study found that each kilogram of weight lost reduced the load on the knee by about 4 kilograms with every step.
  • Losing weight before surgery can reduce the risks of the operation.
  • Keeping to a healthy weight afterwards helps your joint replacement last.

Your GP can refer you to local or NHS weight management support, which may include the NHS Digital Weight Management Programme. Exercise that is gentle on the joints, such as swimming, water exercise and cycling, can help.

How we decide together

I will not judge you on your weight. I will explain your options, the likely benefits and the risks for you, and whether any steps before surgery would make the operation safer. See when is it time for a replacement?
