Pain on the outside of the hip

Pain on the outer side of the hip is very common, especially in women over 40. It is often not caused by arthritis of the hip joint, but by irritation of the tendons on the outside of the hip. With the right exercises, most people get better without surgery.

What is it?

The bony point on the outside of your hip is called the greater trochanter. The tendons of the buttock muscles (the gluteal muscles) attach here, and a small fluid-filled sac (a bursa) sits over them. Pain in this area is called greater trochanteric pain syndrome. It used to be called trochanteric bursitis, but we now know it is usually caused by a problem with the tendons (gluteal tendinopathy) rather than the bursa.

Typical symptoms

  • Pain and tenderness over the bony point on the outside of the hip, sometimes spreading down the outer thigh
  • Pain lying on that side at night, and sometimes lying on the other side too
  • Pain climbing stairs, walking uphill or standing on one leg
  • Pain after sitting with your legs crossed, or getting up from a low chair

How is it different from hip arthritis?

Pain from arthritis of the hip joint is usually felt in the groin and front of the thigh, with stiffness, and difficulty putting on socks and shoes. Pain from the tendons is felt on the outer side of the hip, and is tender to touch. Some people have both. An examination, and an X-ray if needed, can usually tell them apart. See also hip pain or back pain?

Treatments that work best

A trial published in the BMJ compared three approaches in 204 people: education plus an exercise programme, a steroid injection, and “wait and see”.

  • At 8 weeks, 77 in 100 people in the exercise group felt much better, compared with 58 in 100 after an injection and 29 in 100 with wait and see.
  • At one year, the exercise group was still doing best (78 in 100 much better). The benefit of the injection had largely worn off, and it was no better than wait and see.

So, a programme of education and exercise is the best first treatment.

What helps

  • Avoid compressing the tendons: do not sit with your legs crossed, avoid low chairs, and do not stand with your weight on one hip.
  • At night: if you lie on your side, put a pillow between your knees. Lying on the painful side may be uncomfortable for a while.
  • A guided exercise programme with a physiotherapist, to strengthen the buttock muscles gradually. Stretching the outer hip often makes it worse, so it is best avoided.
  • Painkillers and anti-inflammatory gels can help in the short term.
  • A steroid injection can ease severe pain in the short term, and can help you start exercising. On its own, the benefit tends to wear off.

If it does not settle

Most people improve within a few months. If pain continues despite a good exercise programme, I may arrange an ultrasound or MRI scan to look at the tendons. Rarely, a tendon is torn and may need surgery to repair it.
