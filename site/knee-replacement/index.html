Knee replacement in Ipswich

A knee replacement can relieve the pain of knee arthritis and help you walk further and sleep better. I perform total, partial and robotic-assisted knee replacements at Nuffield Health Ipswich Hospital.

Usually 1 to 2 nights

Usually a spinal anaesthetic, sometimes with sedation
Walking
With a frame or crutches, usually on the day of surgery or the next day
Driving

Up to 12 months

What is a knee replacement?

A total knee replacement resurfaces the worn ends of the thigh bone and shin bone with metal parts, with a smooth plastic insert between them. The back of the kneecap is rarely resurfaced. My own research, using National Joint Registry data, found that at 15 years knee replacements did equally well with or without resurfacing of the kneecap, although this can depend on the implant design.

Is it right for you?

A knee replacement is usually considered when arthritis pain limits walking, sleep or daily life, and non-surgical treatments have stopped helping. If arthritis affects only one part of your knee, a partial knee replacement may be an option, and national guidance recommends that suitable patients are offered that choice.

Your first appointment

I will examine your knee, review X-rays and talk through all your options, including physiotherapy, weight management, injections and partial knee replacement where suitable. If we decide together that surgery is right, I will explain the operation, recovery and risks in detail.

The operation

The operation usually takes 1 to 2 hours. Most people have a spinal anaesthetic, sometimes with sedation. Where it is clinically suitable, I can perform the operation with Mako robotic assistance. You will usually be up and walking with the physiotherapists on the day of surgery or the next morning.

Recovery

  • Most people go home after 1 to 2 nights.
  • Regular exercises to bend and straighten the knee are the key to a good result.
  • Swelling and aching are normal for several months.
  • Most people can drive after about 6 weeks, once they can safely make an emergency stop.
  • The knee continues to improve for up to a year.

See the week-by-week knee replacement recovery guide, and read more about preparing for and recovering from your operation.

What results can you expect?

Most people have much less pain and can do more after a knee replacement. Around 1 in 5 people still have some pain or are not fully satisfied, so I will help you weigh this up before you decide.

How long does a knee replacement last?

I use one of the best-performing implant combinations in the UK National Joint Registry, with one of the longest track records. Registry figures show that 95 in 100 of these knee replacements last more than 15 years after surgery, and many last longer. However, individual factors, such as your age and activity level, can affect this significantly.

Risks

Risks include infection (about 1 in 100), blood clots, stiffness (occasionally needing a manipulation under anaesthetic), ongoing pain, wound problems, nerve or blood vessel injury, a fracture around the implant, and wear or loosening that may need further surgery in the future.

Alternatives to surgery

Exercise and physiotherapy, weight loss where appropriate, anti-inflammatory gels and painkillers, and steroid injections for short-term relief can all help. See knee arthritis.

Common questions

How long will I be in hospital after a knee replacement?

Usually 1 to 2 nights. Most people walk with a frame or crutches on the day of surgery or the next day.

How long does recovery take?

Most people drive again after about 6 weeks. The knee keeps improving for up to 12 months. See recovery after a knee replacement.

How long does a knee replacement last?

For the implants I use, National Joint Registry figures show that 95 in 100 last more than 15 years, and many last longer. See how long replacements last.

Could I have a partial knee replacement instead?

Possibly, if arthritis affects only one part of your knee and the ligaments are healthy. I will check this with X-rays at your consultation. See partial or total knee replacement.

How much does it cost privately?

If you are insured, your policy usually covers the consultation, tests and surgery: please check with your insurer and get a pre-authorisation number. If you are paying for yourself, a new consultation with me is £200, and Nuffield Health Ipswich will give you a fixed-price package for the operation once we have agreed your treatment.
