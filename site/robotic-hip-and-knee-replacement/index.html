Robotic-assisted hip and knee replacement

I am trained in Mako robotic-assisted hip and knee replacement, including partial knee replacement, at Nuffield Health Ipswich Hospital. I use it where it is clinically suitable, as one of several good options.

At a glance

Extra step
A CT scan of your hip or knee before surgery
Who operates
I perform the operation; the robotic arm guides it within my plan
Hospital stay and recovery
Similar to a standard hip or knee replacement

How it works

A CT scan before surgery creates a 3D model of your hip or knee, which I use to plan the size and position of the implants. During the operation I guide a robotic arm that helps prepare the bone and position the implants within the limits of that plan. The robot does not operate on its own.

What the evidence shows

Studies show that robotic assistance positions hip and knee implants more precisely and closer to the plan, and some earlier, smaller studies reported less pain and a quicker recovery in the first weeks after robotic knee replacement. Recent large studies have tested whether this leads to better results for patients:

  • RACER-Knee, a UK trial of 339 patients in 10 hospitals, compared Mako robotic-assisted with conventional total knee replacement. The robotic operations were more precise, but at one year there was no significant difference in patients' outcomes, and early recovery and pain were similar (Lancet, 2026).
  • RASKAL, an Australian trial of 334 patients, compared robotic-assisted with computer-navigated total knee replacement and found no significant difference in outcomes at one or two years (Bone & Joint Journal, 2026).
  • A National Joint Registry study of almost 700,000 knee replacements, including more than 22,000 robotic operations, found no significant difference in the risk of revision (further surgery) in the first few years between robotic and conventional total or partial knee replacements (BMJ, 2026).

So when robotic assistance is used routinely, these studies found no significant difference in outcomes at one year, and standard hip and knee replacements remain excellent operations. There may, however, be a case for robotic assistance in more complex surgery, where placing the components accurately is technically challenging.

Is it right for you?

We will decide together at your consultation. It depends on your joint, the type of implant that suits you, and your insurance, as cover for robotic surgery varies between insurers. Read more about hip replacement, knee replacement and partial knee replacement.
