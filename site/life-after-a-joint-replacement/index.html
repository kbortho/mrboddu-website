Life after a hip or knee replacement

Most people get back to the activities they enjoy after a hip or knee replacement, often doing more than before. Here is what to expect, and how to look after your new joint.

Sport and exercise

What you can do after your operation depends partly on your health before it, including your muscle strength, balance and general fitness. The advice below assumes these are normal for your age. I will tailor it to you.

ActivityUsual advice
Walking, swimming, cyclingEncouraged. Swim once your wound has healed, and cycle on an exercise bike from a few weeks.
Golf, bowls, dancingUsually from about 3 months
Doubles tennis, hikingOften possible once you are strong and confident
Running, jumping and contact sportsPut more strain on the joint and may wear it faster. Please discuss these with me.

Keeping active and at a healthy weight helps your joint last. See how long replacements last.

Everyday life

  • Gardening: light gardening from about 6 weeks. A kneeling pad or low stool helps.
  • Kneeling after a knee replacement: it will not damage the knee, but many people find it uncomfortable. It often gets easier with time.
  • Driving: usually after about 6 weeks, once you can safely make an emergency stop.
  • Work: office work often after 4 to 6 weeks, heavier work after about 3 months.

Travel

  • Flying: short flights are usually fine after about 6 weeks. For long-haul flights, it is usually best to wait about 3 months because of the risk of blood clots.
  • Airport scanners: your joint may set them off. You do not need a card or letter. Tell the security staff that you have a joint replacement.

Looking after your new joint

  • Get infections, such as chest, urine or skin infections, treated promptly, because bacteria can occasionally spread to a joint replacement.
  • Tell your dentist and doctors that you have a joint replacement. In the UK, antibiotics are not routinely needed before dental treatment.
  • Look after your bones and reduce the risk of falls, especially as you get older.
  • Seek advice if the joint becomes newly painful, swollen or unstable. See painful or worn joint replacements.
