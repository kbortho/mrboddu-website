Knee arthritis

Knee arthritis (osteoarthritis of the knee) causes pain, stiffness and swelling. Simple treatments help many people, and partial or total knee replacement can help when they no longer do.

Symptoms

  • Pain when walking, on stairs or getting up from a chair
  • Stiffness, especially in the morning or after sitting
  • Swelling, creaking or grinding
  • A knee that gives way or does not fully straighten

Diagnosis

Knee arthritis is usually diagnosed from your symptoms and an examination, with standing X-rays to show which parts of the knee are worn. This matters, because arthritis limited to one part of the knee may be suitable for a partial knee replacement.

Treatment without surgery

  • Exercise and physiotherapy to strengthen the thigh muscles
  • Weight loss if you are overweight
  • Anti-inflammatory gels rubbed onto the knee, and painkillers where needed
  • A steroid injection for short-term relief. See steroid injections.

In line with national guidance, I do not recommend keyhole washouts or hyaluronic acid injections for knee arthritis, because good evidence shows they do not help most people.

When to consider surgery

A knee replacement is worth considering when knee pain has a real effect on your daily life despite these treatments. Depending on the pattern of wear, the options are a partial or a total knee replacement, sometimes with robotic assistance.
